Where to Stay on Maui: Wailea, Kāʻanapali, Kapalua or Kīhei

Updated November 10, 2026

Short answer: Wailea is the safe, sunny pick for a resort trip, with calm morning beaches and Maui's biggest cluster of luxury hotels. Kāʻanapali gives you a long walkable beach and a lively resort strip on the west side. Kapalua is quieter, greener and more exclusive. Kīhei is where to look for condos and lower prices close to good beaches.

Maui's resort areas sit on two coasts that can't be reached quickly from each other. South Maui (Kīhei and Wailea) is about 30 minutes from Kahului Airport (OGG); West Maui (Kāʻanapali and Kapalua) is usually around 45 minutes to an hour. Most visitors pick one side and take day trips: the Road to Hāna, Haleakalā and the upcountry towns are all a drive whichever you choose.

Wailea: polished resorts and dependable weather

Wailea sits on Maui's dry leeward side, in the rain shadow of Haleakalā, so it's one of the sunniest places on the island. A paved coastal walkway links its crescent beaches and big resorts, including the Four Seasons, Grand Wailea, Andaz Maui and Fairmont Kea Lani. Mornings usually bring the calmest water; the wind tends to pick up in the afternoon. For an adults-only stay, Hotel Wailea sits up the hill away from the beach.

It's the easiest choice for a classic resort trip, and the most expensive. Restaurants outside the resorts are a short drive away in Kīhei or the Shops at Wailea.

Kīhei: condos and value next door

Kīhei runs north from Wailea along the same sunny coast. There are few big hotels, but plenty of condominium complexes, many with kitchens, which can bring the cost of a longer stay down. The beaches (Kamaʻole I, II and III among them) are good for swimming and sunsets. Check that any condo rental is a permitted vacation rental. Maui County has been tightening short-term rental rules, and the details change, so confirm before you pay.

Kāʻanapali: the walkable west-side strip

Kāʻanapali was one of Hawaiʻi's first planned resort areas: a long sandy beach with hotels side by side, connected by a beach path you can walk end to end. Puʻu Kekaʻa, better known as Black Rock, at the Sheraton end, is a popular snorkeling spot. In winter you can often see humpback whales from the beach. Expect a breezier afternoon than in Wailea.

Kāʻanapali is just north of Lahaina, the historic town destroyed by the August 2023 wildfire. The Kāʻanapali resorts are open, but Lahaina is still being rebuilt, and much of the burn zone is closed to visitors. Be respectful: it's a place of loss for the people who live there, not a sightseeing stop.

Kapalua: quiet, green and exclusive

Further north, Kapalua is a smaller resort area of headlands, bays and golf courses, home to The Ritz-Carlton Maui, Kapalua and Montage Kapalua Bay. Kapalua Bay is a beautiful, sheltered swimming and snorkeling beach in calm conditions. Being at the northern tip of the island, Kapalua gets more wind and rain than the south side, and winter swells can make the water rough. It suits travelers who want peace over nightlife.

How to choose: quick scenarios

  • First trip, want sun and a resort pool: Wailea.
  • Want to walk to dinner and shops from the beach: Kāʻanapali.
  • Honeymoon or a quiet luxury stay: Kapalua or Wailea.
  • Long stay or tight budget: a condo in Kīhei.
  • Doing the Road to Hāna: any area works; it's a long day either way. Some travelers add a night in Hāna to avoid driving back the same day.
  • Haleakalā sunrise: South Maui is a little closer to the summit road than West Maui. Sunrise viewing requires an advance reservation from the National Park Service.

Budget tips

  • Compare totals. Taxes, daily resort fees and parking can add a lot to the nightly rate at Maui resorts.
  • Consider a kitchen. Food on Maui is expensive. A condo or a suite with a kitchenette for a few breakfasts and lunches can make a difference.
  • Book the rental car early. You'll need one on Maui, and prices rise as dates fill up.
  • Look at shoulder seasons outside the winter holidays and summer school break.
